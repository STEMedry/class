💡 Why Days are Longer in Summer (日長夜短):
Because Earth's axis is tilted 23.5°, the Gold Terminator Ring cuts latitude circles unequally! In June, more than half of the 45°N circle is inside the sunlit hemisphere (~15.4h day), while in December less than half is lit (~8.6h day).

🎓 Sun–Earth–Moon System: Pedagogical & Interactive Guide

🌌 1. Introduction & Core Learning Objectives

The Sun–Earth–Moon system governs our daily cycles, lunar phases, eclipses, and seasonal day-length changes. As Earth rotates on its 23.5° tilted axis every 24 hours, it revolves around the Sun in an elliptical orbit (365.25 days), while the Moon revolves around Earth (29.53 days per synodic month) on an orbit inclined by 5° to the ecliptic plane.
  • Objective 1 (Lunar Phases): Distinguish between the Moon's constant 50% sunlit hemisphere (Terminator) and the changing fraction visible from Earth.
  • Objective 2 (Eclipse Mechanics): Explain why Lunar & Solar Eclipses do not occur every month due to the 5° orbital inclination (enabled by default), occurring only near the Lunar Nodes (March & September).
  • Objective 3 (Seasons & Day/Night Length 日長夜短 / 日短夜長): Understand how the 23.5° axial tilt causes the Terminator Plane to divide latitude circles unequally—creating longer daylight hours in Summer and shorter daylight hours in Winter—even though Earth is closest to the Sun (Perihelion, 147.1M km) in January!
  • Objective 4 (Lunisolar Calendars): Contrast the solar-based 24 Solar Terms (🔶 Diamonds) with lunar-based Synodic Days 1–30 (🔵 Beads).

🧠 2. Pedagogical Design Principles of This Simulation

  • Clean Default Interface & Progressive Scaffolding: Both 🛠️ Learning Support on the left and 📊 Seasonal & Day/Night Data on the right are condensed by default so learners first observe the natural 3D system, then expand tools and data as needed.
  • Click Earth or Sun to Level View onto Horizontal Plane: Clicking directly on the 3D Earth or Sun resets the camera onto the horizontal ecliptic plane, letting learners inspect the 23.5° axial tilt, 5° lunar orbit tilt, or full Solar System orbit edge-on.
  • Counterfactual Experimentation: The Moon's 5° Orbit Tilt is active by default; learners can check ⚖️ Offset 5° Moon Orbit Tilt to flatten the orbit to 0° and see how eclipses would occur every single month without the tilt!

📖 4. Essential Astronomy Terms

Diurnal Arc & Nocturnal Arc (晝弧與夜弧): The sunlit vs. dark segments of a latitude circle cut by the Terminator Ring; their ratio determines day vs. night length.
Subsolar Point (太陽直射點 δ): The latitude on Earth where noon sunlight hits at 90° (shifts between 23.5°N at Summer Solstice and 23.5°S at Winter Solstice).
Lunar Nodes (黃白交點): The two points (Mar & Sep) where the Moon's 5° tilted orbit crosses the Ecliptic Plane.
Terminator vs. Umbra: The Terminator is the day/night circle on a sphere; the Umbra is the dark shadow cone cast into space.
Perihelion & Aphelion: Earth's closest point (147.1M km, Jan) and farthest point (152.1M km, Jul) from the Sun.
24 Solar Terms vs. Synodic Month: Solar Terms divide Earth's orbit into twenty-four 15° segments; a Synodic Month (29.53d) tracks Moon phases.
